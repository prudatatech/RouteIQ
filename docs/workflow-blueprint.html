Operations design · 30 Sep 2026

MargixIndia Workflow Blueprint

Every screen we built works on its own. Together they don't yet tell anyone what to do next. This blueprint defines one flow for moving goods, who owns each step, and what each person sees when they open the app. We rebuild the navigation, home screens and handoffs around it.

What we found

Why it feels disconnected

We audited every page, every role's menu and eight end-to-end journeys in the code. The problems are structural rather than cosmetic:

Menus are organised by feature, not by work. Admins see 22 menu items. Nothing follows the order of the job: receive, dispatch, track, deliver, get paid.
One thing, many names. A vendor's goods appear as "vendor load", "manifest", "consignment", "shipment" and "request" on different screens. Statuses differ per page: "exception" means a failed delivery in one place and a problem case in another.
The same job lives in several places. Assigning a vehicle has three separate screens. Trips are created from the route planner, the optimizer and backhaul. The marketplace is split across Backhaul, Bids and Vendor loads.
Handoffs break between people.
  • Optimized trips sit undispatched with no prompt.
  • Managers can't sign in at all.
  • Several notifications open the wrong page or none.
  • Vendors aren't told a return-trip window opened.
  • Customers never see an invoice.
  • New drivers and uploaded documents reach nobody.
No "what needs me now". The dashboard shows figures, but misses bookings to confirm, bids to decide, shipments with no vehicle and deliveries with no price.
Principles

Seven rules every screen follows

  1. Every role opens on a work queue. Home is "what needs you now", sorted by urgency. Each item has one button that does the next step.
  2. The menu follows the job. Sections run in the order work happens, and each role sees only the sections it works in.
  3. One home page per thing. Every shipment, trip, vehicle, person, case, claim and invoice has one page with its own link. Drawers are for a quick look and always offer "Open".
  4. Every page says what's next. A bar at the top shows the current stage and the one action that moves it forward, or who it is waiting on.
  5. Everything links to what it touches. A shipment links to its trip, vehicle, driver, case, invoice and requester, and each of those links back.
  6. One word per thing, everywhere. The same vocabulary appears in the web app, driver app, customer app and notifications.
  7. Every handoff notifies and deep-links. When a step finishes, the next person is told, and the notification opens exactly the item they need to act on.

Handoffs

Broken links we fix along the way

  • Optimizer to dispatch: optimized trips land in "Trips to send" with a Send button, and the driver is told only when a trip is sent.
  • Vehicle without a driver: assigning one warns you and blocks it. Documents rules apply when the policy says so.
  • New driver signs up, driver uploads a document: staff are told, and the item appears in People → Documents to review.
  • Return trip opens: vendors on that lane are told. If a vendor is missing a location, the vendor is asked to fix it.
  • Invoice issued or paid: the customer, vendor or partner is told. Every invoice links to its shipment and back.
  • Notification links: bookings, KYC, 3PL, claims and vehicle approval all open the exact item. The customer and vendor ids that were missing are added.
  • Manager access: the manager signs in, lands on Today, and never gets a notification it can't open.
  • Dead ends: vendor loads, bids, trips, the live map and invoices all link to the shipment, vehicle, driver and requester they involve.
Delivery plan

Four phases, each shippable on its own

Phase 1 · Staff operations spine first

  • New menu and the Today home
  • One Requests inbox
  • The Dispatch workspace, with one shared assign-vehicle screen
  • A page per shipment with a "what's next" bar and related links
  • Manager access, notification link fixes, and optimizer-to-dispatch

Phase 2 · Money and partners

  • Money section: to price, invoice pages, driver pay, claims
  • Vendor portal rebuilt around My loads, with proofs and claims
  • 3PL portal navigation and approval email
  • Return trips merged into one place

Phase 3 · Mobile apps

  • Driver home shows the one next action
  • Every driver notification deep-links
  • Customer app gets push notifications, invoices, and rating and receipt prompts

Phase 4 · Vocabulary sweep

  • Apply the word list across all apps, notifications and statuses
  • An automated check keeps old words out

Decisions (30 Sep 2026)

  • Driver pay: per trip plus per km. Staff set a fixed amount per trip and a rate per km, per vehicle type. The driver wallet shows real driver pay, not the customer's invoice.
  • Payments: offline for now. Invoices are shown and downloadable in every app. Staff mark them paid when money arrives. A payment gateway can come later.
  • Vendor approval: approve first. A vendor load must be accepted with a price before a vehicle can be assigned.
Based on a code audit of the web app, driver app, customer app and backend on 30 Sep 2026. Phase 1 starts on approval of this blueprint.
